Methodology

Problem and data

This educational system classifies 16 entered indicators into one of three categories found in the CDC Diabetes Health Indicators BRFSS 2015 dataset: no diabetes, prediabetes and diabetes patterns. It is not clinically validated.

Features and data preparation

The pipeline validates ranges, removes exact duplicates and documented invalid rows, and requires all three target classes. Sex, Education, Income, AnyHealthcare and NoDocbcCost are excluded from prediction to simplify the form and reduce dependence on sensitive demographic and socioeconomic attributes. Available excluded columns are used only for offline subgroup evaluation.

Training

All three models use the same 80/20 stratified split (random seed 42). Logistic regression and the decision tree use balanced class weights; histogram gradient boosting receives balanced sample weights for training only. Test records are never resampled.

Evaluation and selection

Evaluation includes accuracy, balanced accuracy, macro and weighted precision/recall/F1, per-class metrics, confusion matrices, multiclass one-vs-rest ROC-AUC, ROC curves and precision-recall curves. Macro F1 is the primary selection metric, followed by macro recall, artifact size and inference time.

Interpretability and group analysis

Coefficient magnitude, tree importance or held-out permutation importance provides global importance depending on model. Importance is association within this dataset, not causation. Sex and broad-age subgroup evaluation is descriptive and does not demonstrate clinical fairness.

Deployment, ethics and limitations

Models and JSON summaries are generated locally and committed for read-only Vercel inference. Requests do not trigger training, network access, database use or filesystem writes. Survey indicators, class imbalance, measurement limitations and population shift all constrain generalization.
